Two different kinds of protection

Avoid the hit

For heroes, physical attacks use the dodge rating and energy attacks use the resist rating. A successful roll prevents the hit. Rating has diminishing returns: more still helps, but the gains get smaller. Already-attached periodic damage does not reroll avoidance.

Reduce a landed hit

General, physical or energy, science-type, and area reductions are fractional modifiers. Applicable factors multiply the damage that remains, then flat armor is subtracted. NPC ratings also reduce damage continuously, so they do not add random misses to hero attacks.

01

Diminishing returns

RATING → PROTECTION
protection = C × r / (r + C × k)

r = applicable defense rating. k = 100 × the authored conversion for the encounter’s difficulty. C = {{ $data.hero_avoidance }} for hero avoidance, or {{ $data.npc_passive }} for NPC rating reduction.

At r = C × k, protection is half its ceiling. Finite ratings approach the ceiling without reaching it. The same rating gives less protection when the difficulty conversion is larger.

Hero resistance, stacking & armor

{{ mul $data.hero_mitigation 100 }}% COMBINED CAP
  1. Apply passive and aura reductions before equipment mitigation.
  2. Multiply the remaining damage by each applicable general, physical/energy, science-type, and area factor. Each factor is 1 − clamp(reduction, 0, {{ $data.hero_mitigation }}).
  3. Subtract the non-negative flat armor for the attack’s physical or energy source.
  4. Enforce one floor relative to the incoming hit, including reductions from earlier stages.

03

NPC defenses & immunity

REDUCTION LIMITS

Ordinary defense: {{ mul $data.npc_passive 100 }}%

Physical hits use the NPC’s dodge rating plus its passive physical defense. Energy hits use its resist rating plus passive energy defense; Turtle suspends that passive energy contribution. The curve produces continuous mitigation, including for periodic damage.

Applicable physical, damage-over-time, and special area reductions multiply afterward. Carapace’s per-hit limit also stays inside the ordinary budget.

ordinary damage ≥ incoming × {{ sub 1 $data.npc_passive }}

Temporary reduction: {{ mul $data.npc_phase 100 }}% total

Timed damage reduction and the specific area-shift defense can lower damage further. Their combined reduction stays within this limit measured against the original hit.

damage after reductions ≥ incoming × {{ sub 1 $data.npc_phase }}

A 1,000-damage hit retains at least {{ mul 1000 (sub 1 $data.npc_passive) }} damage through ordinary defense, or {{ mul 1000 (sub 1 $data.npc_phase) }} through temporary reduction, before later mechanics. This floor does not apply when a scripted immunity blocks the hit.

What sits outside these caps?

Scripted immunity can fully block a hit

NPC charge protection, Turtle protection against physical hits, and directional shields protecting against the attack’s direction now preserve their immunity behavior. They are separate from the {{ mul $data.npc_phase 100 }}% reduction cap, just like intangibility and banishment. Explicit temporary hero immunity is also separate. Scripted defeats still bypass defenses, shields, and phase thresholds.

Absorption, vulnerability & sharing

NPC vulnerability is applied after ordinary and temporary reduction, followed by finite absorption. Damage sharing and encounter health thresholds have separate handling. The reduction floor is not a promise that every hit removes health. PvP honors finite TC shields and does not consume them for rejected attacks.

Why equipment protection changed

The suffix-stat reader previously started five attribute slots too early, which could put Dexterity into damage reduction and shift other modifiers. The corrected suffix offset restores those stats; the server rebuilds its content cache for the change.

Darkspin’s balance policy

The caps and diminishing-return defense curves are server balance decisions. Difficulty conversion comes from authored game tuning. Critical-hit chance is a separate calculation and is not described by this defense curve.

04

Auras, companions & hit reactions

RECENT CORRECTIONS

Crushing Dread uses the strongest aura

Overlapping personal and allied Crushing Dread auras apply once, using the strongest applicable reduction around the attacking enemy. Two 30% auras therefore reduce its damage by 30%, not 51% or 60%. The result remains inside the hero’s combined mitigation cap.

Companion protection has one combined cap

Companions use their own authored defense ratings. Allied aura reduction now shares their {{ mul $data.npc_passive 100 }}% ordinary-defense cap, measured against the hit before the aura. A 1,000-damage hit therefore cannot fall below {{ mul 1000 (sub 1 $data.npc_passive) }} through these combined reductions.

Debuff immunity belongs to the target

In multiplayer, enemy poison, physical and energy vulnerabilities, and control effects check the immunity of the hero actually receiving the effect. Another player’s immunity no longer determines whether that hero receives the debuff.

Thorn Bark reflects the accepted hit

Reflection uses health lost by the surviving hero who was struck. Reflected damage, rewards, and feedback are attributed to that hero in multiplayer. Reflection carries the hero’s position, so enemy directional shields apply correctly. An enemy killed by a damage reaction cannot heal itself afterward through life drain.

Soul Link resolves before a hero replacement

Reserve heroes receive their shared damage before a lethal hit selects a replacement or decides squad defeat. Hit reactions belong to the surviving hero who received the hit, rather than a newly deployed replacement. Defensive on-hit stacks also activate on surviving duel targets that take damage.

Damage classification and catalyst bonuses

Companion attacks carry their own damage classification and position, and Sprout’s poison element reaches resistance checks. Expunge’s single-target remaining-damage burst no longer inherits area mitigation. Catalyst primary-stat bonuses now feed damage and healing; elemental damage and defense-based attack bonuses update with inventory changes, and physical/energy defense-based attack bonuses use their correct attributes.
